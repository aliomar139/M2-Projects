# Top 30 Core Literature: Ranked Core Papers and Defensible Justifications

## 1. Top 30 Selection Methodology and Scoring Rationale

The 30 core papers highlighted in this document were systematically selected from the verified 100-paper corpus based on **ten explicit academic criteria** to establish the foundation for research in **Agentic AI and Machine Learning for Personalized Orthopedic Treatment Recommendation and Clinical Decision Support**.

### The 10 Selection Criteria
1. **Direct Topic Relevance:** Specific focus on orthopedic decision support, individualized treatment selection, or agentic workflow automation.
2. **Relevance to Personalized Treatment:** Focus on patient-specific treatment tailoring rather than one-size-fits-all population averages.
3. **Methodological Rigor & Innovation:** Foundational contributions in causal inference, reinforcement learning, survival analysis, or agent orchestration.
4. **Outcome Relevance:** Explicit investigation of standardized Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (OKS, OHS, KOOS, WOMAC, HAGOS, EQ-5D) or Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID).
5. **Causal & Counterfactual Capability:** Rigorous distinction between factual outcome prediction under observed practice and counterfactual treatment-effect estimation.
6. **Clinical Relevance:** Direct applicability to high-stakes orthopedic clinical dilemmas (e.g., knee/hip arthroplasty vs. conservative care, arthroscopy stratification, fracture recovery).
7. **Validation Quality:** Multi-center cohorts, registry-scale validation (e.g., Danish Hip Registry, OAI, international registries), external temporal validation, or prospective trials.
8. **Recency:** Coverage of the cutting-edge surge in generative AI, LLMs, and agentic paradigms (2020–2026).
9. **Generative & Agentic Paradigm Leadership:** Groundbreaking exploration of LLMs, RAG, tool-using agents, and multi-agent coordination in clinical practice.
10. **Value for Illuminating Research Gaps:** Clear exposure of current boundaries, failure modes, dataset shifts, and implementation bottlenecks in the field.

## 3. Deep Analytical Justification for the Top-30 Core Literature

The 30 papers selected represent six indispensable pillars that collectively define the state of the art and expose the genuine research gaps:

### Pillar 1: Agentic AI & Human-Machine Collaborative Teaming (Ranks 1, 5)
- **Billi & Bini (2026, Rank 1):** Directly introduces Agentic AI into orthopedic surgery in *JBJS*, shifting AI from passive query answering to proactive clinical agents that coordinate multi-step workflows while keeping surgeons in the loop.
- **Woo et al. (2024, Rank 5):** Provides empirical proof in *Arthroscopy* that domain-specific Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) coupled with multi-agent supervision eliminates hallucinations and ensures evidence-based answers.

### Pillar 2: Closed-Loop Dynamic Treatment Regimes & Reinforcement Learning (Ranks 2, 28, 29)
- **Dong et al. (2026, Rank 2):** Couples wearable sensing with dual DRL-TCN architectures to dynamically adapt post-ACLR rehabilitation regimens to individual patient recovery curves.
- **DTR & DRL Foundations (Ranks 28, 29):** Provide the mathematical machinery for off-policy policy optimization and dynamic treatment regimes from observational data.

### Pillar 3: Clinical Decision Prediction vs. Guideline Alignment (Ranks 3, 4, 18, 21)
- **Halvorson et al. (2026, Rank 3):** Demonstrates that LLMs predict surgeon imaging and surgical decisions from previsit intake text with 70–81% accuracy, providing the prime example of *decision imitation*.
- **Dagher et al. (2024, Rank 4):** Evaluates GPT-4 against AAOS clinical guidelines, exposing that ungrounded LLMs fail when balancing multiple comorbid factors.
- **Systematic Review of Surgeon Benchmarks (2025, Rank 18):** Demonstrates across 43 studies that AI matches but does not beat experienced orthopedic surgeons, proving that AI must augment rather than replace clinicians.

### Pillar 4: Foundational Causal Inference & Treatment Recommendation (Ranks 6, 7, 8)
- **Curth & van der Schaar (Nature Medicine 2024, Rank 6):** Establishes why prognostic outcome prediction cannot solve treatment recommendation due to confounding, mandating causal machine learning.
- **DeepSurv (Katzman 2016, Rank 7) & PAI (DeRubeis 2014, Rank 8):** The foundational architectures defining deep survival treatment recommendation and the Personalized Advantage Index.

### Pillar 5: Multicenter PROM & MCID Prediction in Large Joints (Ranks 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 30)
- **Ribbons et al. (2025, Rank 9):** Integrates biopsychosocial features with Bayesian ML across 4 hospitals to output individualized recovery distributions for TKA.
- **Stratified Hip Arthroscopy (Rank 12) & Centre Shift (Rank 30):** High-volume Danish and European registry studies demonstrating stratified care and exposing severe AUROC drops (0.81 to 0.63) across different hospitals.
- **MCID Arthroplasty (Rank 11) & Minimal Feature Optimization (Rank 14):** Prove the clinical necessity of predicting meaningful threshold changes (MCID) using minimal feature burdens.

### Pillar 6: Implementation Science, Human Factors & Clinical Utility (Ranks 15, 16, 17, 22, 24, 25, 26, 27)
- **Methodological Reference Standards (Ranks 15, 16, 17):** Cochrane (2020), Zaniletti (2022), and Vickers (2021) establish PROBAST risk of bias, TRIPOD reporting standards, and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA).
- **Clinical Experience & Human Factors (Ranks 24, 25, 27):** Real-world shoulder CDSS deployments, patient preference integration, and clinician override experiments demonstrating that transparent, preference-sensitive systems are mandatory for clinical adoption.
