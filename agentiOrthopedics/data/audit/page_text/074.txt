ABSTRACT
Introduction: Low back pain (LBP) is a sig-
nificant global health burden, with variable 
treatment outcomes and an unclear underlying 
molecular mechanism. Effective prediction of 
treatment responses remains a challenge. In this 
study, we aimed to develop gene signature-based 
machine learning models using transcriptomic 
data from peripheral immune cells to predict 
treatment outcomes in patients with LBP.
Methods: The transcriptomic data of patients 
with LBP from peripheral immune cells were 
retrieved from the GEO database. Patients with 
LBP were recruited, and treatment outcomes 
were assessed after 3 months. Patients were clas-
sified into two groups: those with resolved pain 
and those with persistent pain. Differentially 
expressed genes (DEGs) between the two groups 
were identified through bioinformatic analy-
sis. Key genes were selected using five machine 
learning models, including Lasso, Elastic Net, 
Random Forest, SVM, and GBM. These key genes 
were then used to train 45 machine learning 
models by combining nine different algorithms: 
Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, Sup-
port Vector Machine, Decision Tree, Random 
Forest, Gradient Boosting Machine, Multilayer 
Perceptron, Naive Bayes, and Linear Discrimi -
nant Analysis. Five-fold cross-validation was 
employed to ensure robust model evaluation 
and minimize overfitting. In each fold, the data-
set was split into training and validation sets, 
with model performance assessed using multi-
ple metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, 
and F1 score. The final model performance was 
reported as the mean and standard deviation 
across all five folds, providing a more reliable 
estimate of the models’ ability to predict LBP 
treatment outcomes using gene expression data 
from peripheral immune cells.

INTRODUCTION
Low back pain (LBP) is a leading cause of disabil-
ity and affects millions of individuals worldwide, 
representing a substantial public health burden 
[1]. It is estimated that over 80% of the popula-
tion will experience LBP at some point in their 
lives, with a significant proportion developing 
chronic or recurrent pain [2 ]. The multifaceted 
components of the lumbar spine, including soft 
tissues, vertebrae, zygapophyseal and sacroiliac 
joints, intervertebral discs, and neurovascular 
structures, are susceptible to diverse stressors 
that can, individually or synergistically, precipi-
tate LBP [3]. According to the Global Burden of 
Disease Study, LBP has consistently ranked as 
one of the top causes of years lived with disabil-
ity (YLDs) globally, contributing to both direct 
healthcare costs and indirect costs related to 
lost productivity and reduced quality of life [4 ]. 
The prevalence of LBP increases with age, and 
as the global population continues to age, the 
economic and social impact of LBP is expected 
to rise further [5].
Despite its high prevalence and burden, 
the etiology of LBP remains complex and

multifactorial, encompassing a wide range of 
biological, psychological, and social factors 
[6, 7]. LBP can be caused by structural issues 
such as herniated discs, spinal stenosis, and 
degenerative disc disease, but in many cases, 
the exact underlying cause remains unclear 
[8]. This uncertainty in diagnosis and the 
variability in patient response to treatments 
complicate the management of LBP. Current 
treatment strategies for LBP include physical 
therapy, pharmacological interventions, and, 
in severe cases, surgical options [9 ]. However, 
these treatments often yield mixed results, and 
a significant proportion of patients continue 
to experience persistent pain despite interven -
tion. The unpredictable nature of treatment 
outcomes for LBP underscores the need for 
personalized approaches to pain management. 
While certain patients may respond well to 
conventional treatments, others may experi-
ence little to no improvement, suggesting that 
individual biological factors play a critical role 
in determining treatment efficacy. Understand-
ing the molecular mechanisms underlying LBP 
and identifying biomarkers that can predict 
treatment response are key challenges in the 
field.
Advances in bioinformatics and machine 
learning techniques offer new opportunities 
to address these challenges by analyzing large-
scale molecular data to uncover the biological 
signatures associated with different pain tra-
jectories [10, 11]. One promising avenue for 
research is the analysis of transcriptomic data 
from peripheral immune cells. Increasing evi-
dence suggests that the immune system plays a 
crucial role in the pathophysiology of chronic 
pain, including LBP [12]. Immune cells, particu-
larly those involved in inflammatory processes, 
can modulate pain pathways through the release 
of cytokines and other signaling molecules [13, 
14]. By studying the gene expression profiles of 
peripheral immune cells, researchers can gain 
insights into the molecular mechanisms that 
may contribute to the persistence or resolu-
tion of pain in patients with LBP. Additionally, 
using machine learning models to analyze gene 
expression data has the potential to identify key 
genes associated with treatment response, pav-
ing the way for the development of predictive 
models that can inform personalized treatment 
strategies for LBP.
Given the significant global burden of LBP 
and the variability in treatment outcomes, this 
study aims to develop a gene signature-based 
machine learning model using transcriptomic 
data from peripheral immune cells to predict 
treatment outcomes in patients with LBP. In this 
study, we developed a comprehensive machine 
learning approach to predict treatment out-
comes in patients with LBP using transcrip-
tomic data from peripheral immune cells. The 
overall workflow consisted of several key steps. 
First, patients with LBP were classified into two 
groups based on their response to treatment 
after 3 months: those whose pain resolved and 
those with persistent pain. Differential gene 
expression analysis was performed to iden-
tify key genes associated with these treatment 
outcomes. Next, five machine learning mod-
els—Lasso, Elastic Net, Random Forest, Support 
Vector Machine (SVM), and Gradient Boosting 
Machine (GBM)—were employed to select core 
genes for model construction. These core genes 
were then used in combination with nine dif-
ferent machine learning algorithms, including 
Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, Deci-
sion Tree, Random Forest, and others, resulting 
in a total of 45 model combinations. The predic-
tive performance of these models was evaluated 
based on accuracy, and SHapley Additive exPla-
nations (SHAP) analysis was used to interpret the 
contribution of each gene to the model’s pre -
dictions. This scheme allowed us to systemati-
cally identify the key genetic factors influencing 
treatment outcomes in patients with LBP and 
provided a robust framework for building pre-
dictive models to inform personalized treatment 
strategies. The detailed procedure is illustrated 
in Fig. 1.
METHODS
Data Source
The transcriptomic data of patients with LBP 
from peripheral immune cells was retrieved 
from the GEO database (GSE177034). This

study utilized transcriptomic data from a 
human low back pain (LBP) cohort, part of the 
PainOMICs project (NCT02037763), funded 
by the European Community under FP7. The 
primary aim was to examine associations 
between genome-wide transcriptomics and 
the development of persistent chronic LBP. A 
retrospective selection of the first 50 patients 
with resolved pain and 50 patients with per-
sistent pain, all white adults, was made. Inclu-
sion criteria included acute LBP (pain level ≥ 4 
on NRS) of < 6-week duration, while exclusion 
criteria ruled out a recent history of persistent 
chronic LBP, spinal fractures, and unstable dis-
ease. Patients received standardized treatment, 
including non-steroidal anti-inflammatory 
drug (NSAID), steroids, and opioids, based on 
clinical signs. Follow-up at 3 months classi-
fied patients into “resolved pain” (NRS < 4) 
or “persistent pain” (NRS ≥ 4) groups. The 
researcher conducting transcriptomic analy -
sis was blinded to patient group assignment.
Ethical Approval
This article is based on previously conducted 
studies and does not contain any new studies 
with human participants or animals performed 
by any of the authors. The transcriptomic data 
of patients with LBP from peripheral immune 
cells were retrieved from the GEO database 
(GSE177034). This study utilized transcrip-
tomic data from a human low back pain 
(LBP) cohort, part of the PainOMICs project 
(NCT02037763), funded by the European Com-
munity under FP7. All procedures performed 
in those studies involving human participants 
were approved by the University Hospital of 
Parma’s Ethics Committee (protocol number 
43543).
Differentially Expressed Gene Analysis
To identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) 
between the persistent and resolved pain groups, 
we performed a differential expression analy-
sis using the normalized gene expression data 
[15]. First, the raw transcriptomic data from the 
GSE177034 dataset were imported and normal-
ized using the normalizeBetweenArrays function 
from the limma package to ensure comparability 
across samples. We then created a grouping vari-
able (group_info) that labeled samples as either 
"Control" (persistent pain) or “Test” (resolved 
pain) based on their clinical status. A linear 
model was fitted to the normalized expression 
data using the lmFit function from the limma 
package, followed by empirical Bayes modera -
tion with the eBayes function to improve the 
statistical power of the analysis [16]. Finally, the 
topTable function was used to extract the list of 
DEGs, applying the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) 
method for multiple testing correction. Genes 
with an adjusted p -value (p.adj) < 0.1 were con-
sidered differentially expressed. The analyses 
were conducted in R (version 4.3.3).

tion, we employed multiple machine learning

training and testing sets with an 80:20 ratio 
[17]. The gene expression data were stand-

ensure that features were on a comparable 
scale. We applied five machine learning mod-

Vector Machine (SVM), and Gradient Boosting 
Machine (GBM)—to select the most important 
genes. The models were trained on the stand-
ardized training data, and feature selection was 
performed using the SelectFromModel function 
[18]. For Lasso and Elastic Net, hyperparam -
eters were set with alpha  = 0.01 to ensure regu -
larization, while Elastic Net had an l1_ratio of 
0.5 to balance L1 and L2 penalties. Random 
Forest and GBM were implemented with 100 
estimators, and SVM was used with a linear 
kernel to perform gene selection. Each model 
identified a set of genes that were important 
for classifying patients with LBP into “resolved 
pain” or “persistent pain” groups. Ultimately, 
these genes were designated as the core features 
for the subsequent predictive model construc-
tion. The final list of key genes was saved for 
further analysis. The analyses were conducted 
in Python (version 3.12).
Machine Learning Modeling
To predict treatment outcomes in patients with 
LBP, we applied nine different machine learn-
ing classifiers to the gene sets selected by five 
feature selection methods. The classifiers used 
were Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, 
Support Vector Machine (SVM), Decision Tree, 
Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Machine 
(GBM), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Naive 
Bayes, and Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA). 
This resulted in a total of 45 machine learning 
model combinations. For each feature selection 
method, the gene subsets were extracted, and 
the corresponding gene expression values were 
used as input features for model training. To 
ensure robust model evaluation and minimize 
overfitting, we implemented five-fold cross-
validation. In this process, the dataset was ran-
domly divided into five equal parts, with each 
part serving as the validation set once while the 
remaining four parts were used for training. The 
features were standardized using StandardScaler 
to ensure that each feature had a mean of 0 and 
a standard deviation of 1. Each classifier was 
then trained on the standardized training data, 
and the models were evaluated on the test set. 
The analyses were conducted in Python (version 
3.12).
Machine Learning Models Evaluation
The performance of each model was measured 
using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score 
[19]. This comprehensive machine learning 
modeling approach allowed us to systematically 
compare the performance of different models in 
predicting LBP treatment outcomes based on the 
selected gene signatures. The SHapley Additive 
exPlanations (SHAP) method was used to visual-
ize the contribution of core genes to the model 
of the first fold cross performance, highlighting 
their roles in predicting treatment outcomes 
[20]. The confusion matrix was used to visual-
ize the predictive performance of the model of 
the first fold cross on the validation set.

select key genes from the transcriptomic data 
of patients with resolved and persistent LBP. 
The results showed that certain genes, such as 
DNASE1L2, CCDC85B, and PTTG1, were con-
sistently selected across multiple methods, 
indicating their potential significance in distin-

Perceptron, Naive Bayes, and Linear Discrimi -

based on four performance metrics: accuracy, 
precision, recall, and F1 score.
As illustrated in Fig. 3, the results of our five-
fold cross-validation showed that certain model 
combinations performed significantly better 
than others. The combination of Elastic Net 
with Logistic Regression achieved the highest

and ± 9.2% respectively.

robust performance across all folds, suggesting 
reliable generalization capability. For instance, 
the Lasso with Linear Discriminant Analysis

the top performers when combined with vari-

distinguishing between patients with resolved

tial biomarkers for predicting treatment out-
comes, offering opportunities for personalized 
interventions in LBP management. Addition-

pain persistence. This comprehensive analysis 
provides a strong foundation for future research 
into tailored pain treatments. The ability to 
accurately predict LBP treatment outcomes using 
gene signatures from peripheral immune cells 
opens avenues for personalized medicine. Iden-
tifying patients likely to experience persistent 
pain could lead to early intervention and tai -
lored treatment strategies, potentially improving 
clinical outcomes and reducing healthcare costs.
DISCUSSION
The Elastic Net with Logistic Regression 
achieved the highest accuracy of 88.7% ± 8.0% 
(mean ± standard deviation), followed closely 
by Elastic Net with Linear Discriminant Analy-
sis (88.7% ± 7.5%) and Lasso with Multilayer 
Perceptron (87.7% ± 6.7%). These models effec-
tively predicted treatment outcomes and dis-
tinguishing between patients who responded 
to treatment and those with persistent pain. 
Furthermore, SHAP analysis provided valu-
able insights into the contribution of individ-
ual genes to model predictions, revealing that 
core genes like DNASE1L2, CCDC85B, SDF2L1, 
UFD1-AS1, and H2AX had significant impacts 
on model performance. These findings under-
score the potential of gene expression data and 
machine learning models in advancing personal-
ized medicine for LBP, enabling earlier interven-
tion and tailored treatment strategies.
The consistent selection of certain genes 
across multiple machine learning methods indi-
cates their potential biological importance in 
distinguishing between patients with resolved 
and persistent pain. Of particular significance, 
DNASE1L2 was identified by all five feature 
selection methods, suggesting a robust role in 
the underlying pathophysiology of pain resolu-
tion. DNASE1L2, a member of the DNase fam-
ily, is involved in apoptotic DNA degradation 
and plays a crucial role in the clearance of cell 
debris during inflammation, which is particu -
larly relevant as efficient clearance of inflam-
matory mediators is essential for preventing 
chronic inflammation and subsequent pain 
persistence [21, 22]. CCDC85B and PTTG1, 
selected by four methods, represent another 
layer of molecular complexity in pain chronic-
ity. CCDC85B has been implicated in the regu-
lation of Wnt signaling and NF-κ B pathways, 
both central to inflammatory responses and pain 
sensitization, while PTTG1, primarily known as 
a securin protein involved in cell cycle regula-
tion, has recently been linked to immune cell 
proliferation and cytokine production [23– 26]. 
Additionally, our analysis identified several 
genes involved in immune cell function and 
inflammation resolution, such as CD28 and 
ICOS for T-cell activation and differentiation, 
and IL1R1 and TNFRSF members for cytokine 
signaling pathways, emphasizing the critical role 
of immune-mediated inflammation in deter -
mining pain outcomes [27–29]. The selection of 
genes involved in cellular stress responses and 
tissue repair (including heat shock protein fam-
ily members and growth factor-related genes) 
suggests that the capacity for cellular adapta-
tion and tissue homeostasis may be key determi-
nants in pain resolution [30–32]. Pathway anal-
ysis of the identified genes reveals enrichment

ing patterns in gene expression data that pre-
dict clinical outcomes in LBP. The Multilayer 
Perceptron showed consistent high performance 
when combined with different feature selection 
methods, including Lasso (87.7% ± 6.7%), Elas-
tic Net (87.7% ± 9.2%), and SVM (85.7% ± 8.5%), 
demonstrating its robust capability in handling 
complex gene expression patterns. This consist-
ency across different feature selection methods 
suggests that neural network-based approaches 
might be particularly suited for capturing sub-
tle relationships in transcriptomic data. On the 
other hand, models involving Decision Trees 
and K-Nearest Neighbors consistently under-
performed, with accuracies ranging from 63.9 to 
77.4%. Decision Tree-based models showed par-
ticularly high variability in performance, with 
standard deviations > 13% in some cases, high-
lighting their instability with high-dimensional 
gene expression data. This notable difference in 
performance underscores the critical importance 
of selecting appropriate machine learning algo-
rithms when working with transcriptomic data 
for clinical prediction tasks.
The high accuracy and robustness of certain 
models, combined with the biological relevance 
of the identified genes, suggest that these gene 
signature-based models could have significant 
clinical applications. In practice, such models 
could be used as part of a diagnostic tool to pre-
dict whether a patient is likely to respond to a 
given treatment or whether their pain is likely 
to persist, allowing for more personalized treat-
ment strategies. For example, patients predicted 
to have persistent pain could be offered more 
aggressive or targeted interventions early in 
their treatment course, potentially preventing 
chronic pain development. In contrast, those 
predicted to have resolved pain might benefit 
from a more conservative approach, minimizing 
unnecessary treatments and reducing health-
care costs. The integration of gene expression 
data with machine learning models represents a 
significant step towards personalized medicine 
in the context of LBP. By identifying specific 
molecular signatures associated with treatment 
outcomes, clinicians could move away from the 
current “one-size-fits-all” approach to a more 
tailored method of pain management. This 
would enable the design of individualized treat-
ment plans that consider each patient’s unique 
molecular profile, leading to improved outcomes 
and a reduction in the burden of chronic pain.
Limitations
Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, 
while the use of gene expression data from 
peripheral immune cells offers valuable insights 
into the molecular mechanisms of low back pain 
(LBP), this approach is limited by the inherent 
complexity of LBP as a multifactorial condition. 
The study focused primarily on immune-related 
gene signatures, potentially overlooking other 
biological processes and tissue-specific pathways, 
such as those related to the musculoskeletal sys-
tem, that could also play significant roles in pain 
persistence and resolution. Second, the sample 
size, although adequate for initial exploration, 
may limit the generalizability of the findings. 
The relatively small number of patients in each 
group (resolved vs. persistent pain) may have 
reduced the statistical power to detect subtle, 
yet clinically important, gene-expression differ-
ences. Larger, independent cohorts are needed 
to validate the identified gene signatures and 
the predictive accuracy of the machine learning 
models. Third, the study exclusively included 
white patients, which limits the generalizability 
of the findings to other ethnic groups. Genetic 
diversity across populations can influence both 
gene expression patterns and the effectiveness of 
predictive models. Future studies should include 
more diverse populations to assess whether the 
identified gene signatures are universally appli-
cable or population-specific. Fourth, while mul-
tiple machine learning models were employed 
to predict treatment outcomes, there is a risk

371Pain Ther (2025) 14:359–373 
of overfitting, particularly with more complex 
models such as Random Forest and Gradient 
Boosting Machine. Overfitting can lead to mod-
els that perform well on training data but fail 
to generalize to new, unseen data. Although we 
applied cross-validation to mitigate this, further 
validation on independent datasets is necessary. 
Lastly, the study focused on predicting outcomes 
after 3 months of treatment. While this time -
frame is relevant for assessing short- to mid-
term responses, it does not capture long-term 
outcomes or the potential for pain recurrence, 
which is common in LBP. Longitudinal studies 
are needed to evaluate whether these gene sig-
natures can predict sustained treatment success 
over longer periods.
CONCLUSION
This study provides a robust framework for using 
transcriptomic data and machine learning mod-
els to predict treatment outcomes in patients 
with LBP. The identification of key genes and the 
development of highly accurate predictive mod-
els highlight the potential for these approaches 
to be integrated into clinical practice. As further 
validation is conducted and models are refined, 
this work paves the way for more personalized 
and effective treatments for LBP, ultimately 
improving patient care and outcomes.
