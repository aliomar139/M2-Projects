Abstract
Introduction
Low back pain (LBP) is a significant global health burden, with variable treatment outcomes and an unclear underlying molecular mechanism. Effective prediction of treatment responses remains a challenge. In this study, we aimed to develop gene signature-based machine learning models using transcriptomic data from peripheral immune cells to predict treatment outcomes in patients with LBP.
Methods
The transcriptomic data of patients with LBP from peripheral immune cells were retrieved from the GEO database. Patients with LBP were recruited, and treatment outcomes were assessed after 3 months. Patients were classified into two groups: those with resolved pain and those with persistent pain. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the two groups were identified through bioinformatic analysis. Key genes were selected using five machine learning models, including Lasso, Elastic Net, Random Forest, SVM, and GBM. These key genes were then used to train 45 machine learning models by combining nine different algorithms: Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Machine, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Machine, Multilayer Perceptron, Naive Bayes, and Linear Discriminant Analysis. Five-fold cross-validation was employed to ensure robust model evaluation and minimize overfitting. In each fold, the dataset was split into training and validation sets, with model performance assessed using multiple metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. The final model performance was reported as the mean and standard deviation across all five folds, providing a more reliable estimate of the models' ability to predict LBP treatment outcomes using gene expression data from peripheral immune cells.
Results
A total of 61 DEGs were identified between patients with resolved and persistent pain. From these genes, 45 machine learning models were constructed using different combinations of feature selection methods and classification algorithms. The Elastic Net with Logistic Regression achieved the highest accuracy of 88.7% ± 8.0% (mean ± standard deviation), followed closely by Elastic Net with Linear Discriminant Analysis (88.7% ± 7.5%) and Lasso with Multilayer Perceptron (87.7% ± 6.7%). Overall, 15 models demonstrated robust performance with accuracy > 80%, suggesting the reliability of our machine learning approach in predicting LBP treatment outcomes. The SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method was used to visualize the contribution of core genes to model performance, highlighting their roles in predicting treatment outcomes.
Conclusion
The study demonstrates the potential of using transcriptomic data from peripheral immune cells and machine learning models to predict treatment outcomes in patients with LBP. The identification of key genes and the high accuracy of certain models provide a basis for future personalized treatment strategies in LBP management. Visualizing gene importance with SHAP adds interpretability to the predictive models, enhancing their clinical relevance.

Introduction
Low back pain (LBP) is a leading cause of disability and affects millions of individuals worldwide, representing a substantial public health burden [1]. It is estimated that over 80% of the population will experience LBP at some point in their lives, with a significant proportion developing chronic or recurrent pain [2]. The multifaceted components of the lumbar spine, including soft tissues, vertebrae, zygapophyseal and sacroiliac joints, intervertebral discs, and neurovascular structures, are susceptible to diverse stressors that can, individually or synergistically, precipitate LBP [3]. According to the Global Burden of Disease Study, LBP has consistently ranked as one of the top causes of years lived with disability (YLDs) globally, contributing to both direct healthcare costs and indirect costs related to lost productivity and reduced quality of life [4]. The prevalence of LBP increases with age, and as the global population continues to age, the economic and social impact of LBP is expected to rise further [5].
Despite its high prevalence and burden, the etiology of LBP remains complex and multifactorial, encompassing a wide range of biological, psychological, and social factors [6, 7]. LBP can be caused by structural issues such as herniated discs, spinal stenosis, and degenerative disc disease, but in many cases, the exact underlying cause remains unclear [8]. This uncertainty in diagnosis and the variability in patient response to treatments complicate the management of LBP. Current treatment strategies for LBP include physical therapy, pharmacological interventions, and, in severe cases, surgical options [9]. However, these treatments often yield mixed results, and a significant proportion of patients continue to experience persistent pain despite intervention. The unpredictable nature of treatment outcomes for LBP underscores the need for personalized approaches to pain management. While certain patients may respond well to conventional treatments, others may experience little to no improvement, suggesting that individual biological factors play a critical role in determining treatment efficacy. Understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying LBP and identifying biomarkers that can predict treatment response are key challenges in the field.
Advances in bioinformatics and machine learning techniques offer new opportunities to address these challenges by analyzing large-scale molecular data to uncover the biological signatures associated with different pain trajectories [10, 11]. One promising avenue for research is the analysis of transcriptomic data from peripheral immune cells. Increasing evidence suggests that the immune system plays a crucial role in the pathophysiology of chronic pain, including LBP [12]. Immune cells, particularly those involved in inflammatory processes, can modulate pain pathways through the release of cytokines and other signaling molecules [13, 14]. By studying the gene expression profiles of peripheral immune cells, researchers can gain insights into the molecular mechanisms that may contribute to the persistence or resolution of pain in patients with LBP. Additionally, using machine learning models to analyze gene expression data has the potential to identify key genes associated with treatment response, paving the way for the development of predictive models that can inform personalized treatment strategies for LBP.
Given the significant global burden of LBP and the variability in treatment outcomes, this study aims to develop a gene signature-based machine learning model using transcriptomic data from peripheral immune cells to predict treatment outcomes in patients with LBP. In this study, we developed a comprehensive machine learning approach to predict treatment outcomes in patients with LBP using transcriptomic data from peripheral immune cells. The overall workflow consisted of several key steps. First, patients with LBP were classified into two groups based on their response to treatment after 3 months: those whose pain resolved and those with persistent pain. Differential gene expression analysis was performed to identify key genes associated with these treatment outcomes. Next, five machine learning models—Lasso, Elastic Net, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM)—were employed to select core genes for model construction. These core genes were then used in combination with nine different machine learning algorithms, including Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and others, resulting in a total of 45 model combinations. The predictive performance of these models was evaluated based on accuracy, and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis was used to interpret the contribution of each gene to the model’s predictions. This scheme allowed us to systematically identify the key genetic factors influencing treatment outcomes in patients with LBP and provided a robust framework for building predictive models to inform personalized treatment strategies. Methods
Data Source
The transcriptomic data of patients with LBP from peripheral immune cells was retrieved from the GEO database (GSE177034). This study utilized transcriptomic data from a human low back pain (LBP) cohort, part of the PainOMICs project (NCT02037763), funded by the European Community under FP7. The primary aim was to examine associations between genome-wide transcriptomics and the development of persistent chronic LBP. A retrospective selection of the first 50 patients with resolved pain and 50 patients with persistent pain, all white adults, was made. Inclusion criteria included acute LBP (pain level ≥ 4 on NRS) of < 6-week duration, while exclusion criteria ruled out a recent history of persistent chronic LBP, spinal fractures, and unstable disease. Patients received standardized treatment, including non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID), steroids, and opioids, based on clinical signs. Follow-up at 3 months classified patients into “resolved pain” (NRS < 4) or “persistent pain” (NRS ≥ 4) groups. The researcher conducting transcriptomic analysis was blinded to patient group assignment.

Differentially Expressed Gene Analysis
To identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the persistent and resolved pain groups, we performed a differential expression analysis using the normalized gene expression data [15]. First, the raw transcriptomic data from the GSE177034 dataset were imported and normalized using the normalizeBetweenArrays function from the limma package to ensure comparability across samples. We then created a grouping variable (group_info) that labeled samples as either "Control" (persistent pain) or “Test” (resolved pain) based on their clinical status. A linear model was fitted to the normalized expression data using the lmFit function from the limma package, followed by empirical Bayes moderation with the eBayes function to improve the statistical power of the analysis [16]. Finally, the topTable function was used to extract the list of DEGs, applying the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method for multiple testing correction. Genes with an adjusted p-value (p.adj) < 0.1 were considered differentially expressed. The analyses were conducted in R (version 4.3.3).
Key Genes Selected
To identify key genes for model construction, we employed multiple machine learning techniques for feature selection using the expression data. The dataset was split into training and testing sets with an 80:20 ratio [17]. The gene expression data were standardized using the StandardScaler function to ensure that features were on a comparable scale. We applied five machine learning models—Lasso, Elastic Net, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM)—to select the most important genes. The models were trained on the standardized training data, and feature selection was performed using the SelectFromModel function [18]. For Lasso and Elastic Net, hyperparameters were set with alpha = 0.01 to ensure regularization, while Elastic Net had an l1_ratio of 0.5 to balance L1 and L2 penalties. Random Forest and GBM were implemented with 100 estimators, and SVM was used with a linear kernel to perform gene selection. Each model identified a set of genes that were important for classifying patients with LBP into “resolved pain” or “persistent pain” groups. Ultimately, these genes were designated as the core features for the subsequent predictive model construction. The final list of key genes was saved for further analysis. The analyses were conducted in Python (version 3.12).
Machine Learning Modeling
To predict treatment outcomes in patients with LBP, we applied nine different machine learning classifiers to the gene sets selected by five feature selection methods. The classifiers used were Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Naive Bayes, and Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA). This resulted in a total of 45 machine learning model combinations. For each feature selection method, the gene subsets were extracted, and the corresponding gene expression values were used as input features for model training. To ensure robust model evaluation and minimize overfitting, we implemented five-fold cross-validation. In this process, the dataset was randomly divided into five equal parts, with each part serving as the validation set once while the remaining four parts were used for training. The features were standardized using StandardScaler to ensure that each feature had a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1. Each classifier was then trained on the standardized training data, and the models were evaluated on the test set. The analyses were conducted in Python (version 3.12).
Machine Learning Models Evaluation
The performance of each model was measured using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score [19]. This comprehensive machine learning modeling approach allowed us to systematically compare the performance of different models in predicting LBP treatment outcomes based on the selected gene signatures. The SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method was used to visualize the contribution of core genes to the model of the first fold cross performance, highlighting their roles in predicting treatment outcomes [20]. The confusion matrix was used to visualize the predictive performance of the model of the first fold cross on the validation set.

Machine Learning Models Evaluation
A total of 45 machine learning model combinations were constructed by combining five feature selection methods (Lasso, Elastic Net, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Gradient Boosting Machine) with nine different classifiers: Logistic Regression, K-Nearest Neighbors, Support Vector Machine, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Machine, Multilayer Perceptron, Naive Bayes, and Linear Discriminant Analysis. These models were evaluated based on four performance metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score.
As illustrated in Fig. 3, the results of our five-fold cross-validation showed that certain model combinations performed significantly better than others. The combination of Elastic Net with Logistic Regression achieved the highest accuracy of 88.7% (± 8.0%), followed closely by Elastic Net with Linear Discriminant Analysis at 88.7% (± 7.5%), indicating strong and stable predictive power. The Lasso and Elastic Net feature selection methods, when combined with Multilayer Perceptron, both achieved accuracies of 87.7%, with standard deviations of ± 6.7% and ± 9.2% respectively.
Different model combinations evaluated. SVM support vector machine, GBM gradient boosting machine
The top-performing models demonstrated robust performance across all folds, suggesting reliable generalization capability. For instance, the Lasso with Linear Discriminant Analysis showed consistent performance with an accuracy of 87.7% (± 5.1%), exhibiting the lowest standard deviation among the top models, indicating stable predictive power. Notably, Multilayer Perceptron consistently appeared among the top performers when combined with various feature selection methods (Lasso, Elastic Net, and SVM), achieving accuracies > 85%.
In contrast, some models showed less effective performance, particularly those involving Decision Trees and K-Nearest Neighbors, with accuracies ranging from 63.9 to 77.4%. The combination of SVM with Decision Tree showed the lowest accuracy at 63.9% (± 13.6%), with high variability across folds. This variability highlights the importance of selecting appropriate classifier and feature selection method combinations.
Overall, our cross-validation results revealed that Elastic Net-based and Lasso-based models, particularly when combined with Logistic Regression, Linear Discriminant Analysis, or Multilayer Perceptron, provided the most reliable predictions for low back pain treatment outcomes. The consistent performance across multiple folds suggests these models could be effective tools for predicting clinical responses in patients with LBP. Discussion
The Elastic Net with Logistic Regression achieved the highest accuracy of 88.7% ± 8.0% (mean ± standard deviation), followed closely by Elastic Net with Linear Discriminant Analysis (88.7% ± 7.5%) and Lasso with Multilayer Perceptron (87.7% ± 6.7%). These models effectively predicted treatment outcomes and distinguishing between patients who responded to treatment and those with persistent pain. Furthermore, SHAP analysis provided valuable insights into the contribution of individual genes to model predictions, revealing that core genes like DNASE1L2, CCDC85B, SDF2L1, UFD1-AS1, and H2AX had significant impacts on model performance. These findings underscore the potential of gene expression data and machine learning models in advancing personalized medicine for LBP, enabling earlier intervention and tailored treatment strategies.
The consistent selection of certain genes across multiple machine learning methods indicates their potential biological importance in distinguishing between patients with resolved and persistent pain. Of particular significance, DNASE1L2 was identified by all five feature selection methods, suggesting a robust role in the underlying pathophysiology of pain resolution. DNASE1L2, a member of the DNase family, is involved in apoptotic DNA degradation and plays a crucial role in the clearance of cell debris during inflammation, which is particularly relevant as efficient clearance of inflammatory mediators is essential for preventing chronic inflammation and subsequent pain persistence [21, 22]. CCDC85B and PTTG1, selected by four methods, represent another layer of molecular complexity in pain chronicity. CCDC85B has been implicated in the regulation of Wnt signaling and NF-κB pathways, both central to inflammatory responses and pain sensitization, while PTTG1, primarily known as a securin protein involved in cell cycle regulation, has recently been linked to immune cell proliferation and cytokine production [23–26]. Additionally, our analysis identified several genes involved in immune cell function and inflammation resolution, such as CD28 and ICOS for T-cell activation and differentiation, and IL1R1 and TNFRSF members for cytokine signaling pathways, emphasizing the critical role of immune-mediated inflammation in determining pain outcomes [27–29]. The selection of genes involved in cellular stress responses and tissue repair (including heat shock protein family members and growth factor-related genes) suggests that the capacity for cellular adaptation and tissue homeostasis may be key determinants in pain resolution [30–32]. Pathway analysis of the identified genes reveals enrichment in several key biological processes, including inflammatory response regulation, cellular stress response and adaptation, immune cell activation and differentiation, apoptotic regulation, tissue repair, and cytokine signaling modulation. These core genes likely represent key molecular signatures that could offer insights into the immune mechanisms driving chronic pain or its resolution, with their identification through multiple machine learning approaches not only validating their biological significance but also highlighting the utility of transcriptomic data in uncovering biological pathways and markers that are not immediately apparent through traditional clinical assessments. The convergence of multiple feature selection methods on these genes strengthens the evidence for their importance in pain chronicity and suggests potential therapeutic targets for intervention in chronic pain conditions.
Furthermore, the study demonstrated that certain machine learning model combinations performed exceptionally well, particularly those involving Elastic Net and Lasso combined with Logistic Regression, Linear Discriminant Analysis, and Multilayer Perceptron. Through five-fold cross-validation, these models consistently achieved accuracies > 87%, with relatively low standard deviations, indicating robust and reliable performance in predicting treatment outcomes based on gene expression data. The ability to accurately classify patients into those who will experience persistent pain versus those who will achieve pain resolution is of great clinical significance, as it could help clinicians tailor interventions more effectively. The success of the Elastic Net and Logistic Regression combination (88.7% ± 8.0%) can be attributed to Elastic Net’s ability to perform variable selection while handling correlated features, effectively identifying the most relevant genes, and Logistic Regression’s capacity to provide stable, interpretable results with good generalization [33, 34]. Similarly, the strong performance of Elastic Net with Linear Discriminant Analysis (88.7% ± 7.5%) suggests that these methods are particularly effective at capturing the underlying patterns in gene expression data that predict clinical outcomes in LBP. The Multilayer Perceptron showed consistent high performance when combined with different feature selection methods, including Lasso (87.7% ± 6.7%), Elastic Net (87.7% ± 9.2%), and SVM (85.7% ± 8.5%), demonstrating its robust capability in handling complex gene expression patterns. This consistency across different feature selection methods suggests that neural network-based approaches might be particularly suited for capturing subtle relationships in transcriptomic data. On the other hand, models involving Decision Trees and K-Nearest Neighbors consistently underperformed, with accuracies ranging from 63.9 to 77.4%. Decision Tree-based models showed particularly high variability in performance, with standard deviations > 13% in some cases, highlighting their instability with high-dimensional gene expression data. This notable difference in performance underscores the critical importance of selecting appropriate machine learning algorithms when working with transcriptomic data for clinical prediction tasks.
The high accuracy and robustness of certain models, combined with the biological relevance of the identified genes, suggest that these gene signature-based models could have significant clinical applications. In practice, such models could be used as part of a diagnostic tool to predict whether a patient is likely to respond to a given treatment or whether their pain is likely to persist, allowing for more personalized treatment strategies. For example, patients predicted to have persistent pain could be offered more aggressive or targeted interventions early in their treatment course, potentially preventing chronic pain development. In contrast, those predicted to have resolved pain might benefit from a more conservative approach, minimizing unnecessary treatments and reducing healthcare costs. The integration of gene expression data with machine learning models represents a significant step towards personalized medicine in the context of LBP. By identifying specific molecular signatures associated with treatment outcomes, clinicians could move away from the current “one-size-fits-all” approach to a more tailored method of pain management. This would enable the design of individualized treatment plans that consider each patient’s unique molecular profile, leading to improved outcomes and a reduction in the burden of chronic pain.
Limitations
Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, while the use of gene expression data from peripheral immune cells offers valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms of low back pain (LBP), this approach is limited by the inherent complexity of LBP as a multifactorial condition. The study focused primarily on immune-related gene signatures, potentially overlooking other biological processes and tissue-specific pathways, such as those related to the musculoskeletal system, that could also play significant roles in pain persistence and resolution. Second, the sample size, although adequate for initial exploration, may limit the generalizability of the findings. The relatively small number of patients in each group (resolved vs. persistent pain) may have reduced the statistical power to detect subtle, yet clinically important, gene-expression differences. Larger, independent cohorts are needed to validate the identified gene signatures and the predictive accuracy of the machine learning models. Third, the study exclusively included white patients, which limits the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic groups. Genetic diversity across populations can influence both gene expression patterns and the effectiveness of predictive models. Future studies should include more diverse populations to assess whether the identified gene signatures are universally applicable or population-specific. Fourth, while multiple machine learning models were employed to predict treatment outcomes, there is a risk of overfitting, particularly with more complex models such as Random Forest and Gradient Boosting Machine. Overfitting can lead to models that perform well on training data but fail to generalize to new, unseen data. Although we applied cross-validation to mitigate this, further validation on independent datasets is necessary. Lastly, the study focused on predicting outcomes after 3 months of treatment. While this timeframe is relevant for assessing short- to mid-term responses, it does not capture long-term outcomes or the potential for pain recurrence, which is common in LBP. Longitudinal studies are needed to evaluate whether these gene signatures can predict sustained treatment success over longer periods.
Conclusion
This study provides a robust framework for using transcriptomic data and machine learning models to predict treatment outcomes in patients with LBP. The identification of key genes and the development of highly accurate predictive models highlight the potential for these approaches to be integrated into clinical practice. As further validation is conducted and models are refined, this work paves the way for more personalized and effective treatments for LBP, ultimately improving patient care and outcomes.
