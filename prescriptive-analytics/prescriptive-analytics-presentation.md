# Prescriptive analytics
### Turning analysis into a recommended action

Using goals, forecasts, and real-world limits to guide decisions

---

<!-- Slide 2 -->
## What is prescriptive analytics?

Prescriptive analytics helps answer:

### “Given what we know, what should we do?”

It compares possible actions against a goal and the constraints that matter.

The output can be a recommendation, ranked options, or an automated action.

---

<!-- Slide 3 -->
## What goes into a prescription?

**A decision:** What choice needs support?

**A goal:** What does a good outcome mean?

**Information:** What is happening, and what may happen next?

**Constraints:** What limits, rules, or requirements apply?

**Possible actions:** What can the organization actually do?

---

<!-- Slide 4 -->
## How it produces a recommendation

1. Define the decision and the outcome to improve.
2. Estimate possible conditions and outcomes.
3. Apply budgets, capacity, deadlines, and rules.
4. Compare feasible actions and their trade-offs.
5. Recommend an action for review or execution.

Optimization searches for actions that best meet the stated goal.

---

<!-- Slide 5 -->
## A simple example: delivery planning

**Goal:** Deliver orders on time while controlling fuel use.

**Information:** Locations, traffic, vehicle capacity, delivery windows.

**Constraints:** Driver hours, road limits, vehicle availability.

**Recommendation:** A route plan that meets the schedule within those limits.

Change the goal or constraints, and the recommended routes may change.

---

<!-- Slide 6 -->
## Where it can help

| Area | Example decision |
|:--|:--|
| Supply chain | How much stock should go to each location? |
| Manufacturing | When should equipment be maintained? |
| Healthcare operations | How should staff and rooms be scheduled? |
| Finance | Which cases should investigators review first? |
| Customer service | How should a limited team prioritize requests? |

Each application connects a real decision to goals and constraints.

---

<!-- Slide 7 -->
## How it differs from the other analytics types

| Type | Main question | Typical output |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Descriptive | What happened? | Reports and summaries |
| Diagnostic | Why did it happen? | Explanations and contributing factors |
| Predictive | What may happen? | Forecasts and probabilities |
| **Prescriptive** | **What action should we take?** | **Recommended or optimized actions** |

Prescriptive analytics uses findings and forecasts as inputs, then evaluates choices against goals and constraints.

---

<!-- Slide 8 -->
## Good recommendations need good judgment

- A poorly chosen goal can produce an undesirable result.
- Incomplete or outdated data can distort the options.
- Recommendations depend on assumptions and constraints.
- People need a way to review decisions and results.

Ask: **What is being optimized, and what might the model be missing?**

---

<!-- Slide 9 -->
## From recommendation to responsible action

Before using a recommendation, make sure the team can:

- understand its main trade-offs;
- approve or challenge the proposed action;
- measure what happened afterward;
- adjust the approach when conditions change.

Automation can fit routine decisions when the rules and oversight are clear.

---

<!-- Slide 10 -->
## The central idea

Prescriptive analytics evaluates possible actions using available information, a defined goal, and real-world constraints.

It gives decision-makers a structured basis for choosing what to do.
